## Decision in brief

The documents identify Nineteen Point Two Ltd, the UK company, as the contracting provider; this review assumes no US entity. The present Terms, DPA and signed Order Form model are a usable starting structure, but **do not use the current document set unchanged for a first US paid customer**. Resolve the DPA's UK Terms URL, verify an actual Order Form and supplier/transfer evidence, and agree a deliverable export/deletion and incident response process before signing. The public site can describe WAIA as UK-operated while it avoids US entity, residency, certification, result and USD price claims. Review the shared notices for the new domain and the actual Tally/analytics configuration promptly.

The priority is the customer data and contract chain. A broad new US legal estate is not the prerequisite. An approved US Order Form and, where needed, a short US privacy/security addendum can carry the specific decisions without replacing the existing company documents.

## Contract and buyer fit

The US Terms §28 select England and Wales law and exclusive courts. That is a legitimate proposal from a UK provider, but US enterprise buyers may require a local forum, arbitration, service of process or a negotiated exception. No dispute escalation or arbitration process is stated. The owner should decide its negotiation floor; counsel should assess enforceability and any mandatory state law that cannot be contracted away. The DPA's UK Terms link creates a more immediate ambiguity than the foreign forum itself.

Terms §23 caps **Nineteen Point Two's** aggregate liability at fees paid or payable in the preceding 12 months and excludes several loss categories. It does not express a reciprocal customer cap, separate security/confidentiality/IP caps, or indemnities. Customer materials carry a warranty in §14 but no explicit defense or indemnity. Those omissions are negotiation choices, not automatic legal defects. They may block procurement or make a one-invoice, short-term deal difficult to price. Obtain owner instructions and targeted legal drafting rather than silently importing a generic US cap or indemnity.

Terms §8 and the [US pricing page](https://waiausa.com/pricing/) defer price, currency and taxes to the signed Order Form. One upfront invoice and 30-day payment are defaults; the term does not renew automatically (§24). The owner must choose quoted currency, who bears bank/conversion charges and applicable sales/use tax or VAT treatment with qualified tax advice; no USD amount, tax result or US entity can be inferred. Decide whether to offer service levels, support hours, procurement evidence, vendor onboarding forms and insurance limits before promising them. The current Security Summary provides no uptime or recovery-time guarantee.

Terms §§12, 25 and DPA §14 promise customer ownership and return/deletion of Customer Personal Data at the customer's choice, with manual export/deletion and protected backups. The application [offboarding playbook](https://github.com/bjcooper23-19-2/workplace-ai-academy-7ebe4f12/blob/687553ff321a1bc92828439863ce5a9ed94aa6a9/docs/operations/customer-offboarding.md) expressly says complete organization export and deletion have not been production validated. That is a delivery risk for a contractual promise, not proof that data cannot be returned or deleted. Define scope, format, timing, retained records, verification and backup cycle in the first deal and validate the process before representing it as routine.

DPA §12 says notify the customer “without undue delay” after awareness of a Customer Personal Data breach. The application has an [incident response playbook](https://github.com/bjcooper23-19-2/workplace-ai-academy-7ebe4f12/blob/687553ff321a1bc92828439863ce5a9ed94aa6a9/docs/operations/security-incident-response.md), but no production exercise was reviewed. US customers may need a negotiated outside notification interval and incident contact; do not promise one until an operator can meet it. The customer, as controller/owner for workforce records, may have its own state notice duty. Nineteen Point Two separately assesses its obligations for its own prospect, account and support data.

US employee context matters even where consumer privacy laws exclude employment records. The product stores identified progress, baseline responses, workflow narratives, administrator review decisions and validation context. Customer employee notices, permitted use, access roles, retention and prohibitions on performance/disciplinary use should be addressed at onboarding. The product should not be sold as passive prompt monitoring, automated employment decision making, a compliance audit, or a guaranteed productivity result. The US buyer should understand that “no surveillance” describes product design, not the absence of identifiable workplace records.

For a US customer sending employee data into the UK-operated service, that inbound movement is **not itself** a UK GDPR restricted transfer. UK rules may govern onward disclosure by Nineteen Point Two to a separate provider outside the UK, including remote access; EU GDPR may also apply to specific EU-origin data and exporters. The DPA §10 is a general commitment to use adequacy or safeguards, **not** a schedule proving which supplier, data, location, transfer tool and assessment applies. The UK ICO distinguishes inbound from restricted outbound transfers and requires active US certification, including HR-data scope, before relying on the [UK Extension to the EU–US DPF](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/adequacy-regulations/how-does-the-uk-extension-to-the-eu-us-data-privacy-framework-work). Confirm the Lovable/Cloudflare/Tally/Resend contracts and support paths; do not present the shared list's Stockholm database as US or exclusive EU residency. No general US cross-border transfer mechanism equivalent to UK GDPR SCCs is assumed for an ordinary US-only workforce deployment; sector, customer and state restrictions still need a case-specific check.

## Minimum credible path and subsequent site edits

1. **Before the next US proposal:** obtain the actual Order Form; choose the exact Terms/DPA URLs or attach the signed versions; approve England and Wales forum or a negotiated alternative, currency, taxes, payment, term, liability, indemnity and support positions. Do not present an unsigned web page as a completed customer agreement.
2. **Before first US employee data:** recheck current Lovable/Supabase region, Cloudflare and Resend/Tally processing, supplier contracts and transfer tools; validate a scoped export/deletion rehearsal and an incident contact/runbook exercise. Give the customer a short data inventory, current subprocessor list, security limits and employee notice responsibilities. Add state-specific processor language only when buyer coverage and use call for it.
3. **For the live US site:** after legal/owner choices, change the US Terms introduction and links only to the selected contracting route; update Data & Privacy to explain UK operation, known processing locations and limits without a residency promise; align Privacy/Cookie links with `waiausa.com/analytics-preferences/` if a US beacon is active, and make the UK Visibility Check explicitly UK-only. Review the Tally form's “organisation” spelling, privacy link, confirmation and `us_*` submission. Keep pricing enquiry-led until currency/tax are approved. Replace UK/EU examples in the AI statement with a jurisdiction-neutral boundary or accurate US examples, while retaining the human-review, no-training and no-compliance-guarantee language. No public correction should precede the owner/legal decision it depends on.
